# Mapa Gusinja – Gusinje city map on the AR cube

Part of **Xplore Prokletije**.

Hold the printed AR cube in front of your phone and the town of Gusinje stands on it. Aim the ring at a building and hold still, or tap its dot, and the building pushes up out of the cube through the cracking stone, with an enamel name sign hanging on the side that faces you. Turn the cube upside down (or tap **Grad / Town**) to go back to the map.

**Open:** https://wave84mn.github.io/city-map/ (Chrome on Android, Safari on iPhone; the page must be opened over https for the camera).

## How it plays
- **Town on the cube.** The town is the 3D Gusinje jigsaw map (1:4500, ground ×2.5, landmarks ×2.5), 2.8 times as wide as the cube by default, twice the area of the earlier 2× (*Settings → Town size*, 1.4–4.5×; phones that had the old default saved move to the new one). Its south edge turns towards you when the cube is first found. Whichever cube face points up carries the town (phone tilt sensor).
- **Dots.** Gold dots are buildings with a detailed model; white dots are other landmarks (name only, model later).
- **Hand in the air** (on by default; *Settings → Hand in the air*). Put the cube on a table, hold the phone in one hand and use the other in front of the camera, above the town (v0.12, matching how the user tried it in the 7 Nov video):
  - pinch thumb and index finger to take hold of the map;
  - while holding, spread them to zoom in and close them to zoom out (like two fingers on the screen; zoom = start × (gap / gap at the pinch, at least 2.5 cm), with a small dead zone, 1–5×), and move the hand to move the map: the map point between the fingers stays between them;
  - hold still for about a second, or take the hand out of the picture, to let go: the map stays as it is; pinch again for the next move;
  - two hands that both pinch stretch or squeeze the map between them;
  - **pinch a dot to open its building** (v0.13, from the user's 3rd 7 Nov video, where he pinched the dots): a quick pinch (let go within 0.5 s) or a pinch held still for 0.6 s while a ring fills around the fingers, within 36 px of the dot; moving the hand more than 24 px first turns it into a normal move/zoom. The map doesn't move or zoom while a dot is being picked, and what letting go zoomed is undone. Tapping the dot on the screen also works. ("Bring the hand closer to zoom" is gone.)
  - closing the fingers never zooms below the zoom at the pinch (the fingers keep closing for a moment after the pinch is seen).
  The town then shows in a frame where the whole map was (the town size, 2.8× the cube's width by default): zoomed in you see part of the town, bigger, and the cut sides show earth. Past the town's edge the map gives a little and springs back. **Whole map** (top right) glides back to the whole town. Your real hand is drawn in front of the town (cut out of the camera picture along the tracked fingers), with a cursor between thumb and index finger that fills blue while you hold the map. While hand tracking is on, holding still does not choose a building (the phone is held still on purpose while the other hand works); the ring still names what is in the middle.
  - **What the hand tracker needs (v0.11, from the 7 Nov phone video).** MediaPipe finds a hand only when it can see the palm: a hand reaching in right in front of the lens, with only the fingers in the picture, is never found. The hint under the map now says what the tracker sees: *Show your whole hand above the town, 30–50 cm from the phone* (no hand) → *Hand found · pinch thumb and index finger to grab the map* → *Drag the map · bring your hand closer to zoom · open your fingers to let go* (holding), or *The hand holding the cube doesn't move the map* when the only hand in sight is on the cube.
  - **The hand that holds the cube is ignored.** In the video it was the only hand ever found, and its grip read as a pinch (thumb–index 1–4 cm): it grabbed the map and once opened Čekića džamija by itself. A hand counts as holding the cube when at least two finger joints lie on the cube's sides on screen (on or next to its outline, not over its top face and not beyond the top's far side) and it is not much nearer to the camera than the cube (palm < 1.9 × the cube's size on screen), but only while the cube is being moved: its tilt against gravity (tilt sensor) changed by more than 3° within a second, in the last 2 s, or no tilt sensor. A cube resting on a table or stand is never held, so a pinch over the near part of the town (which on screen covers the cube's front) always works there. A holding hand can't grab, isn't drawn over the town, and must open its fingers again before it can pinch.
  - **A pinch must start from open fingers**: thumb and index more than 4.8 cm apart at least once, then closed under 3.2 cm on two readings. A hand that turns up already closed (a fist, a grip) never grabs. A hand seen with its fingers open on three readings in a row counts as a free hand and is never taken for the one holding the cube (v0.12: on a lap the cube moves, and the user's free hand over it was blocked).
  - Detection thresholds 0.4 / presence 0.35 / tracking 0.35 (were 0.5 / 0.45 / 0.45), so a found hand stays tracked while part of it leaves the picture.
  - **The field of view no longer changes under the fingers.** The camera's field of view is learned from the cube and was re-applied whenever the estimate moved by 0.3°; each change shifts the picture and restarts the pose smoothing, so during a pinch (on the screen or in the air) the map slipped. Now changes wait until the map is let go, and once learned only changes of 1° or more are applied.
  Tracking runs in a worker with MediaPipe Hand Landmarker (GPU where the phone allows, otherwise CPU); it loads about 19 MB the first time (`js/vendor/mediapipe`, `models/hand_landmarker.task`). `?hands=off` starts without it, `?hands=cpu` forces the CPU.
- **Fingers on the screen (v0.10).** Two fingers zoom the map (spread to zoom in up to 5×, pinch to zoom out); the points of the map you touched stay under your fingers, like a map app. One finger slides the map. **Whole map** (top right) brings the whole town back. On a computer the mouse drags and the wheel zooms around the pointer. A drag or pinch never counts as a tap on a dot, holding still never chooses while fingers are on the screen, and a hand in the air lets go when fingers touch. The map does not turn with the fingers (the cube turns it). The page itself can't scroll or zoom over the map (`touch-action: none`, Safari's gesture events blocked).
- **Choosing.** The ring in the middle snaps to the nearest dot and shows its name. Hold the cube steady with a gold dot inside the ring for 1.4 s and the building is chosen. Holding only counts while the cube is still; there is a 2 s pause after the town appears, and the building you just left can't be chosen again until the ring has moved off it. You can also tap a dot or a name in the list at the bottom. *Settings → Choose by holding still* turns holding off.
- **Building appears out of the floor (v0.14).** A blue ping marks the building's spot on the map, the map sinks into the stone block, the block's top cracks open with blue light from below, and the building pushes up through it at full size, door towards you: it shakes a little and stretches as it shoots up, glows where it comes out of the floor (its cut face glows too, so it never looks hollow), throws stone chips (the ones near the edge tumble off the block) and a ring of dust, and lands with a small squat and a shock ring (2.5 s, `js/rise.js`, timeline in `RISE`). Going back runs it in reverse: the building sinks into the floor and the map comes back up.
- **Name sign (v0.14).** An enamel street sign (deep blue, white rim, glossy clear coat) with a pictogram of the building type (mosque, church, tower house, clock tower, town hall, sebilj), the name in the app's language, the other two names under it, and the jigsaw piece (map square) like a house number. It hangs on two iron rings from pins on the side of the block that faces you, drops down from over the edge and clacks against the stone; when you walk round the cube it swings up and drops again on the side you now face. On the flat card it hangs from a small wooden frame in front of the building. Different from the museum cube's engraved brass plate.
- **Building.** Buttons: back to town, turn 90°, face me, about (names in all three languages and its jigsaw piece code; the short story comes later).
- **Back.** Turn the cube upside down (two quarter-rolls within 2.5 s count too), or tap the back button. Turning it over during the grow reverses it.
- **Languages:** Montenegrin/Bosnian, Albanian, English (ME / SQ / EN). The first visit follows the phone's language.
- Works with the printed 70 mm cube, the 60 mm paper cube or the flat paper card (same marker codes as ar-cube).
- **Camera (v0.7.1).** Some phones hand the browser a zoomed-in picture (a 16:9 crop, a default zoom, or even the telephoto lens): everything then looks far too close and a hand next to the cube is out of the picture. The app asks for the sensor's own 4:3 picture at 1× zoom with continuous focus; on Android it moves to the main back camera ("camera2 0") if Chrome opened another lens; and when the phone has several back cameras a camera button (top right, with the lens number) switches lenses, remembered on the phone. When the whole 4:3 picture is shown with room around it, a soft dark copy of the picture fills that room.
- **The right lens, found by measuring (v0.7.2).** On the user's phone Chrome opens a lens with about 19° along the long side, while the phone's camera app has about 69° (measured on the cube in the user's two videos): everything looks about 4× too big. As soon as the cube shows two faces, the app measures the lens; narrower than 40° and the phone has other back cameras, it moves to the next one by itself ("This camera is too narrow (19°) – trying the next one…"), measures that, and keeps the widest normal lens ("Camera 2: 65° – this one is good"). Measured lenses are remembered, so next time the good one opens straight away. A lens that never shows the cube within 7 s is skipped. With only one back camera it zooms out if the camera allows, otherwise it says the browser only gets a narrow camera. *Settings → Camera* lists the back cameras with their measured angles; picking one there (or with the camera button) turns the search off, "automatic" turns it back on. Tested with two fake back cameras (narrow by default): it switched by itself and kept the 65° one.
- **Field of view, learned.** When two or more faces of the cube are seen, the camera's field of view is worked out from how the rigid cube fits (from Gusinjska kocka, `js/cv/calib.js`); the median of recent estimates is used and saved per camera. *Settings → Camera angle* shows it and can fix it by hand ("back to automatic" undoes that); `?fov=` fixes it from the link. *Settings → Tracking details* shows the lens, zoom and field of view.
- **Cube tracking (v0.8): OpenCV in the background.** The cube's markers are found with OpenCV's ArUco detector (OpenCV.js 4.x, WebAssembly, the same code Gusinjska kocka uses), and both the detector and the pose solver run in a background worker (`js/cv/worker.js`). The page grabs a camera frame, sends the part to search, and keeps showing that exact frame until its pose comes back, so the town never swims against the picture, while drawing, touches and the hand tracker keep the page to themselves. The next frame goes to the worker before the current one is drawn. OpenCV (13 MB, about 3.7 MB as downloaded) starts loading in the background as soon as the page opens; until it is ready, the app's own detector runs on the page, and it stays if OpenCV can't load. If the worker can't start, OpenCV runs on the page instead. `?det=own` keeps the own detector, `?worker=off` runs OpenCV on the page. On the benchmark frames (`gusinjska-kocka/test/gen_bench.py`, 40 per condition) OpenCV finds more markers everywhere: good light 98 → 100 %, close 73 → 99 %, far 71 → 89 %, dim 50 → 82 %, green faces 86 → 97 %, green and dim 13 → 40 %; identical in JavaScriptCore (Safari's engine, run with Bun) and V8. Same printed cube, no reprint.
- **iPhone and Android safety nets (v0.8).** The camera video plays inline and muted on the page (Safari pauses videos that aren't). A picture that hasn't changed for 1.5 s (video paused, ended or not moving on two checks in a row; a page that was only busy drawing doesn't count) is restarted by itself (paused video: play again; camera stopped in the background: reopened); if that needs a tap, "The camera picture has stopped – tap the screen" shows. Coming back to the page or from the back/forward cache restarts the camera. If frame callbacks don't arrive within about 1.5 s, frames are read on every screen refresh (`?loop=raf` forces this). three.js is loaded by relative paths, so the app no longer needs import maps (Safari before 16.4). The hand tracker starts only after the cube has been found and OpenCV has settled, and it is switched off if the phone takes the 3D drawing away for lack of memory. On an iPhone, inside Instagram, Facebook and similar apps, the start screen says to open the page in Safari.
- **Tracking tolerance:** a pose is accepted when its fit error is under 12 % of the markers' size on screen (3 % for a single marker), and the last pose is kept for 0.5 s through short gaps. A hand-folded paper cube is 1–2 mm off the ideal shape, which at 20 cm is 10–15 px; the old fixed limit (3.5–6 px) dropped the cube about a third of the time in the first phone test.
- The map has a green base underneath (the jigsaw pieces have no undersides). Shadows are drawn only under a building, not under the town (faster).

## Files
- `index.html`, `css/app.css`, `js/app.js`: the app. `js/i18n.js`: all texts in three languages.
- `js/cv/`: own marker detector and pose solver from **ar-cube** (wave84mn/ar-cube); `tracker.js`, `calib.js` and `cvdetect.js` (OpenCV ArUco detector and its loader) from **Gusinjska kocka** (bertiscus/gusinjska-kocka), the loader extended to work in a worker; `worker.js`: the background tracking worker. `data/layout.json`: the cube's marker layout (same as ar-cube and Gusinjska kocka).
- `js/vendor/opencv/`: OpenCV.js (Apache 2.0, WebAssembly embedded), the same file as Gusinjska kocka's `ar/opencv.js`.
- `data/town.glb`: the 64 jigsaw pieces from **Slagalica Gusinje** (wave84mn/gusinje-slagalica, `data/pieces.glb`): millimetres, Y up, −Z north, vertex colours (alpha 128 marks water), meshopt-compressed, about 0.27 M triangles.
- `data/city.json`: the 13 landmarks (names in three languages, jigsaw piece, position on the map in mm) and which model belongs to each.
- `js/rise.js`: the building pushing up out of the floor (cracks, light, chips, dust, the building material's glow) and the name sign.
- `models/<id>.json` + `.jpg`: building models from **Gusinjska kocka** (bertiscus/gusinjska-kocka); `nova-dzamija.json` and `kuca-radoncica-balica.json` are built here (`tools/models/nova_dzamija.py`, `kuca_radoncica_balica.py`, shapes in `buildkit.py`) and have a colour per vertex instead of a texture (GKM1 header `col: true`, no `.jpg`); GKM1 format (quantized mesh as base64 JSON) with a 1024 px baked texture; Y up, base at y = 0, door towards +Z.
- `print/`: the cube print files and the paper cube/card PDF (from ar-cube).
- `img/`: the Xplore Prokletije logo without its background: `xplore-prokletije-light.png` (original colours, for white paper) and `xplore-prokletije-dark.png` (light tower, lifted blue, for dark screens). Made by `tools/logo_prep.py`, which splits each pixel into dark ink, blue ink and paper so edges stay smooth. The app's accent colour is the logo's blue.
- `print/qr/`: QR cards with the Xplore Prokletije logo in the middle and "Mapa Gusinja" below: `mapa-gusinja-qr-android.png` opens `…/city-map/?p=android`, `mapa-gusinja-qr-windows.png` opens `…/city-map/?p=windows` (error correction H; 300 dpi, about 14 × 18 cm, scale down freely; a 3 cm wide code still scans). Made by `tools/qr_cards.py` (segno).
- On Android outside Chrome (a scanner app's built-in browser, Samsung Internet…) the start screen offers **Open in Chrome** (an intent link). On a computer, or with `?p=windows`, the start text talks about the computer's camera.
- `js/vendor/three`: three.js r184 (MIT).
- `js/hands.js` (hand tracker front end, pinch detection, drawing the hand and the cursor), `js/hand-worker.js` (MediaPipe in a module worker), `js/vendor/mediapipe/` (MediaPipe Tasks Vision 0.10.35, Apache 2.0; where the files come from and their hashes are in its README), `models/hand_landmarker.task` (MediaPipe Hand Landmarker, float16 v1).

## Adding a building
1. Make the model: a scan with a photo texture with `tools/models/convert.py` (Gusinjska kocka's converter, copied here), a scan with vertex colours (RealityScan's OBJ export) with `tools/models/convert_vc.py tools/models/models.json <id> --out models`. Raw scans go in `tools/models/scans/` (not committed). `convert_vc.py` can cut the building out of its surroundings (`keep` outline, `box`, `ground_below`), straightens the scan and turns the façade named by `front` to +Z; `--preview-only` writes a quick five-view picture to check the cut and the turn, and every run writes a scan-vs-game check sheet to `tools/models/tmp/<id>/`. Nova džamija (v0.14.1): the scan itself, cut out and shrunk, looked like a ruin (lumpy walls, holes, blotchy colour, ragged base), so the mosque was rebuilt from exact shapes with the scan's measurements (`tools/models/nova_dzamija.py`): prayer hall with the dome on an octagonal drum, the five-arch porch with a side arch, the side wing, the open pavilion with columns, a fountain and the small dome, the minaret on its square base with the balcony and the lead cone, framed windows, the door, cornices, a stone plinth and a low platform. Colours from the scan (white plaster, lead-grey domes with faint seams). 7 300 triangles, 183 KB, no texture.
   Kuća Radončića – Balića (v0.15): the scan is a coloured point cloud (no surface). Measured in a frame square to the walls: 12.25 × 10.35 m, stone ground floor to 2.85 m (laid as stones of varied size and shade), wooden floor band, white plastered upper floor to 5.0 m with wooden windows, steep hipped roof (about 40°) of grey shingles with eaves 13.85 × 12.3 m at 5.3 m and a 2.6 m ridge at 10.45 m, gutters and downpipes, the stone annex with the red double door and metal pent roof at the front, the lean-to porch over the back door, on a small garden plot. 2 600 triangles, 115 KB. `convert_vc.py --preview-only` writes the measured orthographic views used for it.
2. In `data/city.json`, add `"model": "<id>"` to its landmark (or add a new landmark with `id`, `piece`, `name` {me, sq, en} and `pos` [x, y, z] in map millimetres; x east, z south, y a little above the roof).

## Tests (tools/)
Run from a working folder that has ar-cube's `build/` (layout and face images, from `layout.py`):
- `seq.py still|flip|orbit OUT.y4m [--fov DEG]`: synthetic camera video of the cube (uses `synth.py` from ar-cube); `--fov 23` makes a telephoto-like camera (the app learns about 19.5° on a 720×1080 crop of it and puts the cube at 943 mm, true 945).
- `e2e.cjs VIDEO.y4m OUT dwell|nohold|select|flip`: runs the app in Chromium with that video as the camera and a simulated tilt sensor: hold-to-choose, tapping a dot, the list + back button + language switch, and turning the cube upside down. Run `dwell` with `?hands=off` as the last argument: while the hand tracker runs, holding still doesn't choose.
- `frames.cjs VIDEO.y4m OUT <landmark>`: screenshots of the building coming out of the floor frozen at 0–100 %.
- `film/rise-demo.js`: a 10 s clip in the film harness (`FILMSEG=tools/film/rise-demo.js node tools/film/film.cjs OUT`, then `tools/film/encode.sh OUT`): the map sinks, Nova džamija pushes up, the name sign drops, the camera goes round and the sign moves to the next side, then back to the town; it checks those steps and prints `RESULT: PASS`.
- `flip.cjs FLIP.y4m`: choose Opština, then the video turns the cube upside down: records the modes and up faces and checks building → shrink → town.
- `touch.cjs VIDEO.y4m`: two-finger zoom (touched points stay under the fingers), one-finger slide, Whole map, a drag from a dot doesn't open it, a tap does, mouse wheel zoom, tracking details not saved.
- `recover.cjs VIDEO.y4m`: pauses the camera video, ends the camera track, and fakes a return to the page; the app must pick the camera up again and find the cube each time.
- `bench-detect.mjs . BENCHDIR` (node or bun): marker recall of the own detector and OpenCV on still frames; "outside gt" counts markers found on faces the ground truth leaves out (steeper than 72°), not wrong ids.
- With the debug view (`?debug` or *Settings → Tracking details*), marker outlines are drawn on the picture and a small text box sits under the top bar (tap it to shrink it to one line; remembered on the phone). Since v0.10 the switch is never saved: the details show only with `?debug` in the link or for the visit when switched on in Settings. Its line starting `det` shows the detector (`worker`, `main` or `own`), whether OpenCV is ready and how long it took to load, the worker's time per frame, the delay from camera frame to drawn frame, the frame loop (`rvfc` or `raf`) and `PAUSED` if the browser paused the camera video. `__city.eng` gives the same to tests.
- `film/test-hands.js`: the hand gestures with made-up hand readings, run in the film harness: `FILMSEG=tools/film/test-hands.js SHOTS="zoom:40,two:62" node tools/film/film.cjs OUT`. It checks that the hand gripping a lifted, tilting cube is recognised and never grabs, taps or is drawn, that a hand arriving already closed doesn't grab until it opens, that with the cube resting a pinch over the town's near part grabs, the live hints, that a grabbed point stays under the fingers while dragging and zooming, that the hand zooms by coming closer, two-hand stretching, the springy edge, a quick pinch opening a building and **Whole map** (24 checks), and prints `RESULT PASS`.
- `topview.html?cx=&cz=&w=` (with `topshot.cjs OUT.png cx cz w`): the map from above with a millimetre grid and the dots, for finding where a new landmark goes. In the app, `__city.probe(x, z)` gives the map height and jigsaw piece at a spot.
Serve the folder first, e.g. `python3 -m http.server 8123`.

## Showcase video (tools/film/)
A vertical 1080 × 1920 promo clip (about 37 s, Montenegrin captions) filmed from the real app in headless Chromium:
- `film.cjs OUTDIR`: steps the app frame by frame on a virtual clock and takes a screenshot of each frame (JPEG). `SHOTS="B-town:60,C-choose:120"` renders only those frames, for checking.
- `director.js`: the script, loaded into the app page. It draws the "camera picture" (a wooden table with the printed cube, same markers as `data/layout.json`), moves the phone and the cube, hands the cube's pose and the tilt reading to the app through `__city.film`, and lays the captions, title and QR ending on top (`film.css`). Everything else is the normal app: the town, the ring and hold-to-choose, the list, turning the cube over, the three languages.
- `music.py`: a soft synthesised sound track (pad, plucks, bells, whooshes) timed to the events `film.cjs` writes; `qr_square.py`: the QR code at the end (the app's link, logo in the middle).
- `encode.sh OUTDIR`: one H.264 file per segment, joined, plus the sound → `OUTDIR/mapa-gusinja-showcase.mp4`.
In software rendering a frame with the whole town takes about 4 s, so the full film takes roughly 45 minutes.
